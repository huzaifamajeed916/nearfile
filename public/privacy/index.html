Your files never leave your device

Nearfile has no server that receives files. Every tool runs inside your browser. This page explains what that means and how you can check it.

How the tools work

When you add a file, your browser reads it from your device into the memory of the open tab. The conversion code runs in that same tab, and the finished file is handed back to you as a download.

The file, and anything made from it, is never sent to Nearfile or anyone else. There is nowhere to send it: Nearfile is a set of plain web pages with no server-side code.

What Nearfile keeps

Nothing about your files. There are no accounts, cookies, analytics or ads. The only thing saved is your light or dark theme choice, kept in your browser’s local storage on this device.

Other services involved

Two outside services help deliver the site. Neither ever receives your files.

  • The web host that serves these pages. Like any website host, it may keep standard access logs, such as IP address, browser type and the page requested.
  • jsDelivr, a public content delivery network that supplies the open-source libraries the tools are built on: pdf-lib, PDF.js, JSZip and SortableJS. Your browser downloads these code files from it, and Nearfile asks the browser not to say which page you came from. To draw some PDFs correctly, PDF.js may also download standard font or character-map files from jsDelivr. These are generic files, the same for everyone. Your PDF is never part of the request.

Each library is pinned to an exact version and checked against a fingerprint (Subresource Integrity) before it runs, so your browser would refuse a file that had been tampered with. The pages also carry a Content Security Policy that only allows them to load from this site and jsDelivr.

When your files are cleared

Files and results live only in the tab’s memory. Choosing Remove all, starting over, reloading or closing the tab clears them. Files you download are saved by your browser like any other download and stay on your device.

Check it yourself

  1. Open any tool and let the page finish loading.
  2. Turn off Wi‑Fi, or switch on airplane mode.
  3. Convert a file. It still works, because nothing needs to be sent anywhere.

Prefer to look under the hood? Open your browser’s developer tools, choose the Network tab and convert a file. No request carries your file.
