    How to compress a PDF

    1. Add your PDF. Drop it on the page, or tap Select PDF.
    2. Choose a method. Keep text shrinks the pictures inside and leaves the words alone. Flatten pages turns every page into a picture, for the smallest files.
    3. Pick a strength. Balanced suits most PDFs. Choose Strong when you need it smaller still.
    4. Compress and download. You’ll see the size before and after. If a setting can’t make the file smaller, you’ll be told instead of getting a bigger file.

    Questions and answers

    Is my PDF uploaded anywhere?

    No. Nearfile opens and compresses the PDF inside this browser tab. Nothing is sent to a server, and closing the tab clears everything. Once the page has loaded, it even works offline.

    Which method should I use?

    Start with Keep text and Balanced. PDFs with photos or scans usually shrink a lot, and the text stays sharp and searchable. If it’s still too big, try Strong. For a scan where each page is one big picture, Flatten pages often gives the smallest file.

    Why didn’t my PDF get much smaller?

    Most of a PDF’s weight is in its pictures. A PDF exported from a document or spreadsheet is mostly text and lines, which are already tiny, so there’s little to remove. Check whether it holds large photos or scans before expecting big savings.

    What does Flatten pages do to my text?

    Each page is drawn as a picture and the PDF is rebuilt from those pictures. Text can no longer be selected, copied or searched, and links and form fields stop working. Use it for scans and for files you only need to read or print, not for documents people need to fill in.

    Does compressing lower the quality?

    Strong and Balanced re-compress pictures the way JPG does, which loses a little detail. That’s fine for reading on screen and for most printing. Keep your original if you need full print quality: Nearfile makes a new file and never changes yours.

    What stays the same with Keep text?

    Page sizes, text, lines and shapes, links, bookmarks and form fields. Only the pictures and the file’s inner structure change. Digital signatures don’t survive, because any change to a signed PDF breaks its signature.

    Can I use a password-protected PDF?

    Not yet. If a PDF needs a password to open, or is locked against editing, open it in your PDF reader, save a copy without the protection, and use that copy.
